10. Emergency Call Protocol

PRIORITY: Emergency calls override everything. Respond immediately.

Step 1 — Recognise Emergency

  • Caller mentions: severe pain, bleeding, labour pain, loss of consciousness, difficulty breathing, convulsions, child not responding, accident
  • Caller is distressed, speaking fast, or a family member is panicking
  • Any caller mentioning "emergency" or "urgent"

Step 2 — First Response (within 5 seconds)

🎧FIRST RESPONSE

"Sir / Ma'am, please come to Aastha Hospital immediately. Our team is available. Address: L-2/50, Opposite Kangra Niketan, New Mahavir Nagar, Tilak Nagar, New Delhi – 110018. You can also call this same number when you arrive — our reception will direct you. How far are you from the hospital right now?"

Step 3 — Keep Calm, Keep Caller Calm

  • Speak slowly and clearly — your calm transfers to the caller
  • Give the address and number first, don't over-question
  • If ambulance needed: "I am alerting our team immediately. Please stay on the line."
  • Escalate to the on-duty supervisor immediately while the caller is still on the line
🎧PREGNANCY EMERGENCY

"Ma'am, please come to Aastha Hospital immediately — do not wait. Our gynaecology & labour room team are available. L-2/50, Opposite Kangra Niketan, Tilak Nagar, New Delhi. Is someone with you who can bring you?"

🎧CHILD EMERGENCY

"Please come to Aastha Hospital immediately. We have NICU, PICU and 24-hour paediatric support. Come straight to the emergency entrance."

🎧ADULT MEDICAL EMERGENCY

"Please come to Aastha Hospital immediately or send the patient now. L-2/50, Opposite Kangra Niketan, New Mahavir Nagar, Tilak Nagar, New Delhi. Our emergency team is available. Do not delay."

Step 4 — Internal Escalation After Emergency Call

  • Immediately alert on-duty supervisor
  • Inform the relevant clinical department if time permits
  • Document the call: caller name, patient condition, time, action taken
DO NOT put an emergency caller on hold. DO NOT transfer without staying on the line. If you must transfer, stay connected until the receiving person confirms they have the caller.

11. Difficult Call Handling

A. Angry or Frustrated Caller

🎧RESPONDING TO AN ANGRY CALLER

"I completely understand your concern, [Name], and I sincerely apologise for the inconvenience you have experienced. Let me make sure this is addressed for you right away. Could you give me a moment — I am noting down the details. I am escalating this to the relevant team now. You will receive a callback within [X hours]. Is this number the best one to reach you?"

KEY PRINCIPLE: Do not argue. Do not defend. Acknowledge — empathise — escalate.

B. Caller Asks for Doctor's Personal Number

🎧RESPONSE

"Our doctors' personal numbers are not shared — all appointments and queries go through the hospital helpline. I can book an appointment with [Doctor] right now, or note an urgent message for [him/her]. Which would you prefer?"

C. Caller Threatens Legal Action

🎧RESPONSE

"[Name], I hear you and I take your concern very seriously. I am immediately escalating this to our senior management. You will receive a call from our medical director / hospital manager within [timeframe]. May I have the best contact number?"

Stop the call here. Do NOT engage in argument. Escalate to Medical Director. Document exact words used.

D. Media / Journalist Calling

🎧RESPONSE

"Thank you for calling Aastha Hospital. For media queries, I will need to transfer you to our administration team. May I take your name and contact number?"

Do NOT speak to media on behalf of the hospital. Do NOT confirm or deny any event. Escalate immediately.

E. Caller in Distress — Mental Health / Emotional Crisis

🎧RESPONSE

"I am glad you called. I am here with you. Could you tell me what is happening — I want to help you. Aastha Hospital has a psychiatry and counselling team. I would like to book an urgent appointment for you today or tomorrow. Would that be okay?"

Speak slowly and gently. If caller expresses self-harm intent — stay on the line, connect to supervisor immediately, offer an emergency appointment.

12. Complaint Handling — SOP

Step 1 — Receive and Acknowledge

🎧RESPONSE

"Thank you for bringing this to our attention, [Name]. I sincerely apologise for your experience. What happened is not the standard we aim for. I am noting down everything you have told me."

Step 2 — Record the Complaint

  • Caller name and contact number

  • Department and doctor name (if applicable)

16. Dos and Don'ts — Call Conduct

DO

  • Answer within 3 rings — every time, no exceptions
  • Greet warmly using the standard greeting script
  • Ask for the caller's name early and use it
  • Listen completely before responding
  • Read back all appointment details for confirmation
  • Speak at a comfortable pace — not too fast
  • Stay calm and composed even with angry callers
  • Escalate immediately when required
  • Log every call in the CRM before moving to the next call
  • Thank the caller at the end of every call

DO NOT

  • Put an emergency caller on hold — ever
  • Make clinical judgements ("aapko ye bimari hai")
  • Quote specific rates without authorisation from billing
  • Share any doctor's personal mobile number
  • Argue with a caller — even if they are wrong
  • Guarantee cashless or panel approval
  • Say "We do IVF at Aastha" — IVF is at Maccure Dwarka only
  • Disconnect an unhappy caller without escalating
  • Leave a call unlogged in CRM
  • Speak negatively about any other hospital or doctor

18. Final Notes for Call Centre Team

Every call is someone's moment of need. They are not calling out of curiosity — they are worried about their health or their family's health. Your voice, your words and your speed make a real difference to them. Treat every call as if your own family member is on the other end.

  • When in doubt — escalate. Always.
  • When a patient is distressed — slow down your speech, not your urgency.
  • When a call is difficult — the answer is empathy first, solution second.
  • Your goal is not to end the call. It is to give the caller a clear next step before you end the call.
